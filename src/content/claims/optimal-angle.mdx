When an attacker is running parallel to the sideline, deep in the opponent's half, they face a geometric dilemma:
- If they shoot from too far away, the distance gives the goalkeeper too much time to react.
- If they dribble too close to the goal line, the angle becomes too narrow, effectively making the goal "shrink" to zero.

There is a single, mathematically optimal point along their run where the visible angle of the goal is at its absolute maximum.

## The Trigonometric Setup

Let the goal line be the x-axis, and the goal width be $W$. The attacker's distance from the goal line is $x$, and their line of approach is at a perpendicular distance $D$ from the near post.

The visible shooting angle $\theta$ is the difference between the angles to the far post and the near post:
$$ \theta = \theta_{far} - \theta_{near} $$

## The Optimization Calculus

To maximize $\theta$, we can maximize $\tan(\theta)$ using the tangent difference identity:
$$ \tan(\theta) = \frac{\tan(\theta_{far}) - \tan(\theta_{near})}{1 + \tan(\theta_{far})\tan(\theta_{near})} $$

Substituting our geometric values ($\frac{D+W}{x}$ and $\frac{D}{x}$):
$$ \tan(\theta) = \frac{\frac{D+W}{x} - \frac{D}{x}}{1 + \left(\frac{D+W}{x}\right)\left(\frac{D}{x}\right)} = \frac{Wx}{x^2 + D(D + W)} $$

To maximize this function, we can divide the numerator and denominator by $x$, then apply the AM-GM inequality to find that the denominator is minimized when:
$$ x = \sqrt{D(D + W)} $$

This simple equation proves that the optimal distance to shoot from the goal line is exactly the geometric mean of your distances to the near and far posts along the x-axis.
