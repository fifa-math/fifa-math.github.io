When a team goes down to 10 men, they are mathematically at a severe disadvantage. However, the probability of them losing is entirely dependent on the variable $t$, the minute the red card was issued.

## Survival Analysis

We can model the probability of a team holding onto a draw or a win using survival analysis. If a team receives a red card in the 10th minute, they must "survive" for 80 minutes against a superior force. 

The conditional probability of winning given a red card at time $t$ can be expressed as:

$$ P(Win | Red Card, Minute=t) $$

If $t < 20$, the win probability plummets to roughly 15%, while the loss probability skyrockets. The defending team is eventually mathematically overwhelmed by the physical toll of covering the extra space. 

However, as $t$ approaches 90, the impact of the red card diminishes exponentially. A red card in the 88th minute barely alters the expected outcome of the match, as there simply isn't enough time ($90 - t$) for the opposing team to capitalize on the spatial advantage.
