How do we measure the "Atmosphere" of a stadium? Most people assume it's directly correlated to the raw number of fans present. However, acoustic engineers and psychologists model this very differently.

## The Logarithmic Return of Raw Volume

If you place 10,000 fans in a massive 80,000-seat stadium, the raw decibel output is high, but the *perceived atmosphere* is awful. This is because the relationship between raw fan count and atmospheric pressure follows a **Logarithmic Curve**. 

The jump from 1,000 fans to 10,000 fans creates a massive spike in perceived noise. But the jump from 60,000 fans to 70,000 fans offers severely diminishing returns.

$$ Atmosphere_{raw} \approx \log(Fans) $$

## The Exponential Power of Density

To create an intimidating atmosphere, **Density (Capacity Percentage)** is mathematically far more powerful than raw volume. 

A 10,000-seat stadium packed to exactly 100% capacity traps acoustic energy. The physical proximity of the fans creates an exponential feedback loop of crowd chanting (social contagion).

$$ Atmosphere_{density} \approx e^{(Fans / Capacity)} $$
