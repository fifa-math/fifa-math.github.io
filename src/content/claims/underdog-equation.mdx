Soccer is famously a low-scoring sport, which makes it statistically highly susceptible to variance. You can explain this using a Poisson distribution, which models the probability of a given number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time. 

Soccer's scoring frequency can be modeled using the Poisson distribution. 
The probability of a team scoring $x$ goals is defined as:

$$ P(x) = \frac{\lambda^x e^{-\lambda}}{x!} $$

Where $\lambda$ represents the team's expected goals (xG). Below is an interactive visualization of the variance.

Because $\lambda$ (average goals per game) is typically between 1.0 and 1.5, the variance is massive. A dominant team with an xG of 2.5 can easily draw or lose against a lesser team with an xG of 0.5 simply due to statistical noise, making soccer mathematically the most favorable team sport for underdogs.
