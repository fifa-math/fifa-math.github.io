If height is an advantage in winning aerial duels, shouldn't a team of 11 incredibly tall players mathematically dominate the sport? The answer is no, because football is a game of shifting spatial geometry.

## The Biomechanics of Center of Gravity

Height provides a massive **Expected Value (EV)** advantage during set-pieces (corners and free kicks) where the ball is airborne in the penalty box. Therefore, Center Backs and Strikers are statistically drafted from the extreme right-tail of the human height distribution. 

However, in the midfield, the ball is played on the ground in extremely tight spaces. Here, biomechanics dictate that a lower center of gravity allows for faster rotational turning speeds and shorter stride-recovery times. Therefore, the optimal Playmaker (e.g., Lionel Messi at 170cm) is drafted from the left-tail or center of the distribution.

## Positional Distributions

If we look at title-winning teams, their squad doesn't cluster around one average height. Instead, they require a wide standard deviation, tightly grouped by positional roles.
