When fans buy a ticket to a soccer match, they want to see goals. But do they want to see *too many* goals? 

Data collected from fan satisfaction surveys over the last decade perfectly map onto a **Gaussian Distribution** (or Normal Curve).

## The Mathematics of Tension

The enjoyment of a football match isn't driven strictly by the total number of goals scored; it is driven by *competitive tension*. 

If a match ends 0-0 or 1-0, the tension is high, but the payoff (goals) is too low, leaving fans slightly unsatisfied. 

However, if a match ends 9-0, the payoff is incredibly high, but the competitive tension drops to absolute zero after the 4th goal. The mathematical probability of the losing team mounting a comeback disappears, causing the spectator's psychological investment to plummet.

The optimal viewing experience—the absolute peak of the Gaussian curve—hovers around **4.5 total goals**, specifically in competitively balanced distributions like a 3-2 or a 2-2 draw.
