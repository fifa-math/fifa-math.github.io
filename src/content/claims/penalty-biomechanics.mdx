When a player steps up to take a penalty kick, the entire goal frame represents a probabilistic matrix of success. However, human anatomy means that not all areas of the goal are equally accessible to the striker.

## The Biomechanical Default

For a right-footed player, the physical mechanics of striking a football make it significantly easier to generate extreme rotational power when swinging their leg *across* their body. 

If a right-footed striker swings naturally, the ball's trajectory defaults to the left side of the goal frame (which is the **Goalkeeper's Right**). 

Conversely, to place the ball in the opposite corner (the Goalkeeper's Left), the striker must artificially "open up" their hips and strike the ball with the inside of their foot. While this can be done with great accuracy, it mathematically reduces the maximum possible velocity ($v$) of the strike, giving the goalkeeper precious milliseconds to complete their dive.

## Visualizing the Matrix

If we plot thousands of penalty kicks taken by right-footed players, a distinct **Bivariate Distribution Heatmap** emerges. The "hot spots" are heavily biased toward the Goalkeeper's Right side.
