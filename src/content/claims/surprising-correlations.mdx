If possession doesn't guarantee a win, what does? We can use the coefficient of determination, $R^2$, to measure how much of the variance in a team's points per season can be explained by a single metric.

$$ R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2} $$

Here are some of the most surprising statistical correlations found when analyzing decades of soccer data.

## The High Correlations

These metrics are mathematically proven to drive success:

1. **Wage Bill vs. League Position ($R^2 \approx 0.70$)**: The harsh reality of modern football is that money dictates outcomes. The correlation between a club's total wage bill and their final league position is incredibly high.
2. **Expected Goals (xG) Difference vs. Points ($R^2 \approx 0.85$)**: Over a 38-game season, a team's xG Difference (xG created minus xG conceded) is the single most accurate predictor of their total points, filtering out the noise of lucky finishes or spectacular goalkeeping.

## The Surprisingly Low Correlations

These metrics often dominate punditry but have little mathematical bearing on the final score:

1. **Distance Covered vs. Points ($R^2 \approx 0.05$)**: Running more does not mean winning more. In fact, teams that dominate possession often run *less* because they make the opposing team chase the ball. 
2. **Corners Won vs. Goals Scored ($R^2 \approx 0.10$)**: Despite the roar of the crowd when a corner is won, the statistical conversion rate of a standard corner kick into a goal is less than 3%. Accumulating corners is a terrible predictor of attacking success.
