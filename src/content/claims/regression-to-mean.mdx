At the start of every season, a relatively unknown striker will score 6 goals in their first 4 matches. Pundits will declare them a breakout superstar. By December, that same striker will go 10 matches without scoring a single goal. Pundits will declare them a "one-season wonder" who lost their confidence.

The math tells a much simpler, unemotional story: **Regression to the Mean**.

## The Law of Large Numbers

Expected Goals (xG) is a metric that assigns a mathematical probability to every shot taken, based on historical data. If a striker has accumulated 10.0 xG over a season, they "should" have scored 10 goals.

In a small sample size (like 4 matches), statistical variance is incredibly high. A striker might score 6 goals from only 1.5 xG. This is an unsustainable overperformance. As the sample size grows toward the full 38-game season (The Law of Large Numbers), the extreme variance disappears. 

To correct the early overperformance, the universe mathematically forces a "goal drought"—where the striker might score 0 goals from their next 3.0 xG. By the end of the season, their actual goals scored will almost perfectly mirror their underlying xG baseline.
