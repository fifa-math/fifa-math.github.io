When you watch a highlight reel, it's easy to assume that long-range screamers, direct free kicks, and spectacular diving headers make up a significant portion of soccer's goals. However, the mathematical distribution of goal types tells a very different, much more practical story.

## Relative Frequency of Goal Types

By analyzing thousands of goals across the top five European leagues, statisticians can build a categorical distribution (or discrete probability distribution) mapping out exactly how goals are scored.

- **Inside the Box (Open Play)**: Over 50% of all goals are scored from inside the 18-yard box using the feet during open play. This reinforces the "Geometry of the Goalkeeper" concept—getting closer to the goal exponentially increases the visible angle and scoring probability.
- **Outside the Box**: Despite the highlight-reel nature of long-range shots, they only account for roughly 12% of goals, largely due to the mathematical constraints of human reaction time and the size of modern goalkeepers.
- **Dead Balls & Penalties**: Penalties make up a surprisingly high ~9% of goals, while direct free kicks (curling over the wall) sit at a mere 4%.
