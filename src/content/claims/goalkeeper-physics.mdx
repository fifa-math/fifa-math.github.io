When a striker shoots from inside the 6-yard box, they gain a massive mathematical advantage. Not only does the angular size of the goalmouth increase exponentially (giving the striker more room to miss the goalkeeper), but they also completely break the biological limits of human reaction time.

## The Kinematics of a Penalty Kick

A standard penalty kick is taken from 12 yards (11 meters) away. A professionally struck penalty travels at an average speed of 70 mph (31.3 m/s). 

Using basic kinematics ($t = d/v$), the ball will reach the goal line in roughly **350 to 400 milliseconds**.

## The Biological Limit

The average human visual reaction time is about **250 milliseconds**. This is the time it takes for the eye to see the ball move, the brain to process the trajectory, and the central nervous system to send a signal to the leg muscles. 

However, a goalkeeper must also physically propel their 80kg body through the air to reach the corner of the net, which takes an additional **500 to 700 milliseconds**. 

Mathematically, it takes a goalkeeper almost 1,000 milliseconds (1 second) to see a ball and dive to the post. The ball arrives in 400 milliseconds. 

Therefore, a "Reaction Save" on a well-struck penalty is scientifically impossible. Goalkeepers must commit to diving *before* the ball is even struck. This is known as an **Anticipation Save** (or "Guessing"), relying entirely on the statistical probability of the striker's habits and micro-expressions in their run-up.
