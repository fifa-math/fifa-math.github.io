Soccer is universally considered the world's game, played in over 200 countries. Yet mathematically, the distribution of wealth, talent, and viewership is fiercely concentrated in a tiny geographic footprint.

## The European Oligopoly

When we map the top 50 clubs in the world by revenue, attendance, and global TV viewership, a stark pattern emerges. Despite the sport's global reach, the financial gravity of the sport pulls almost exclusively toward Western Europe, specifically England, Spain, Germany, and Italy.

This geographic concentration acts as a mathematical feedback loop:
1. Higher local viewership and legacy attendance generate massive television broadcast contracts.
2. The broadcast revenue allows these clubs to buy the best players in the world from South America and Africa.
3. The accumulation of the world's best players attracts an even larger *global* TV viewership, further enriching the same concentrated group of clubs.

By spinning the globe above, you can visually observe how the financial weight of the entire sporting world rests on just a few dozen coordinate points.
