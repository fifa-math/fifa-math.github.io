In international soccer, home-field advantage usually accounts for a baseline win probability of around **53.7%**. However, when teams travel to play in La Paz, Bolivia—the highest capital city in the world at 3,600 meters (11,800 feet) above sea level—that mathematical baseline is destroyed.

## Hypoxia and Probability

At extreme altitudes, the air density is significantly lower, resulting in physiological *hypoxia* (oxygen deprivation) for unacclimatized players. The mathematical impact on the game is staggering.

Statistical analysis of decades of South American World Cup qualifiers reveals a direct linear regression between the altitude differential of the two teams and the home win probability. When an unacclimatized lowland team (like Brazil or Argentina) plays in La Paz, the home win probability for Bolivia skyrockets from the baseline 53.7% to an incredible **82.5%**. 

Furthermore, for every additional 1,000 meters of altitude difference between the two teams' home environments, the home team's goal differential mathematically increases by approximately **0.5 goals**.
