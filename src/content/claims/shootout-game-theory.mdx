When a knockout match goes to a penalty shootout, it is traditionally conducted in an **ABAB format** (Team A shoots, then Team B shoots). This creates a massive mathematical flaw in competitive fairness. 

## The Psychological Matrix of Game Theory

In an ABAB shootout, the team that shoots first (Team A) is mathematically shielded by a psychological safety net. When Player A steps up, they are almost always shooting to *take the lead*. If they miss, the score simply remains tied. 

However, because Team A has a 73% chance of scoring their penalty, Team B is almost always stepping up to shoot while *trailing*. This induces a psychological matrix state known as "Elimination Pressure." 

The math is brutal. When a player is shooting to tie the shootout (or keep their team alive), their historical conversion rate plummets from 73% down to roughly **62%**. 

Because this pressure stacks and cascades down the order, Team A (the team that shoots first) wins approximately **60%** of all penalty shootouts.
