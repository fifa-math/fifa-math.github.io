When Johan Cruyff said, "Football is a game of space," he was describing a mathematical concept that modern analysts now measure using **Pitch Control**.

Instead of just tracking where the ball is, Pitch Control models calculate the probability that a player from a specific team could reach any given spot on the pitch before an opponent.

## Voronoi Tessellation

The most fundamental mathematical way to visualize this space is using a **Voronoi Diagram**. A Voronoi diagram partitions a plane into regions based on distance to points in a specific subset of the plane. 

In soccer, the points are the players. The region assigned to a player is the area of the pitch that is mathematically closer to them than to any other player on the field.

By integrating player velocity vectors (speed and direction) gathered from optical tracking cameras, analysts can warp these Voronoi cells to calculate exactly which team "owns" the space. A team can have only 30% possession of the ball, but dominate 60% of the valuable Pitch Control zones, choking out the opponent's passing lanes.
