Why do some fans happily travel 3 hours to sit in the freezing rain to watch a 0-0 draw, while others refuse to attend a sunny home game? 

The decision to attend a match is a complex multivariate equation that balances **Positive Vectors** (pull factors) against **Negative Friction** (push factors).

## The Six Axes of Matchday Experience

We can plot the matchday experience across six primary axes:

1. **Atmosphere**: The acoustic density and passion of the crowd.
2. **Match Tension**: The competitive stakes of the game (e.g., a relegation battle vs a meaningless mid-table friendly).
3. **Sightlines**: The physical geometry of the stadium. Tracks and massive dugouts destroy sightlines.
4. **Ticket Value**: The perceived financial fairness of the entry fee.
5. **Travel Ease**: The logistical friction of public transit or parking.
6. **Weather Comfort**: The physical elements.

## Visualizing the Friction

Using a **Radar Chart** (or Spider Chart), we can visualize the total "volume" of a matchday experience. A highly enjoyable match maximizes the surface area of the polygon, pushing far out on the positive axes while minimizing the drag from logistical friction.
