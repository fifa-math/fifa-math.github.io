When Jurgen Klopp brought "Gegenpressing" to the forefront of modern soccer, analysts needed a way to objectively measure how intensely a team was pressing their opponents. You can't just count tackles, because a team that dominates possession won't have the opportunity to make many tackles.

Enter **PPDA** (Passes allowed Per Defensive Action).

## The Formula

The formula isolates actions in the opponent's defensive and middle thirds of the pitch:

$$ \text{PPDA} = \frac{\text{Opponent Passes}}{\text{Defensive Actions (Tackles, Interceptions, Fouls)}} $$

A **lower** PPDA means the defending team is highly aggressive, allowing very few passes before disrupting the play. A team with a PPDA of 7 is deploying an exhausting, high-octane press. A **higher** PPDA (like 15 or 18) indicates a team that is sitting back in a low block, allowing the opponent to pass the ball freely in their own half.

Because high pressing requires immense physical stamina, PPDA is frequently used to track team fatigue over 90 minutes. You will mathematically observe a team's PPDA rise sharply in the final 15 minutes of a match as exhaustion sets in and their pressing lines break down.
