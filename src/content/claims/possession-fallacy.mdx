Pundits love to talk about possession. "They dominated the ball," they say, pointing to a team holding 70% possession. However, mathematically, raw possession percentages have a surprisingly weak correlation with actually winning games.

## The Math Behind the Fallacy

If we take the possession percentage $x$ and the win rate $y$ of teams across a season, we can calculate the Pearson correlation coefficient ($r$):

$$ r = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}} $$

When we run this on top-flight European leagues, $r$ often hovers around 0.3 to 0.4. This is a weak positive correlation. Why? Because a team holding 70% possession in their own defensive third generates exactly zero expected goals (xG). 

Instead of raw possession, mathematicians look at **Field Tilt**, which measures the share of possession strictly in the final third.
